# Project controls

Read the section matching the complication; apply only controls needed for actual or imminent work.

## Conflicting or partial revisions

Distinguish external originals, editable working versions, the selected basis for a stated purpose, and exact issued versions. Preserve original names, issuer IDs, revision labels, messages and package relationships. Filenames and modification times do not establish applicability or precedence.

A later receipt may replace a whole package, one item, selected answers or nothing. Determine its reach from contents and correspondence. Keep an explicit reference to each source supporting an assembled basis; do not call a reconstructed set an original receipt. A chosen basis may be provisional and does not establish customer acceptance or contractual precedence.

For internal files, use a simple revision/date convention that distinguishes materially different versions, including same-day variants if needed. Do not formally revise for every cosmetic edit. Historical decisions must reference the version discussed, not a link that always opens today's mutable file. Preserve the original decision's meaning when recording a correction or change.

## Multiple parties and assumptions

Keep customer requirements, supplier offers and exclusions, internal provisional decisions, and actual communications distinct. One party's answer does not settle another party's agreement. A partial answer resolves only the part supported by evidence; record what remains open and its consequences.

For a live issue, retain only fields useful to the work: affected item, source, material party positions, current decision or assumption, next action or party awaited, affected outputs, and known date. Use a stable existing identifier when possible. A few issues belong in the guide; introduce one combined register only when repeated exchanges make it easier to maintain.

An assumption needs its purpose and what will confirm or replace it, when known. A pricing assumption does not authorize execution. Before release, assumptions affecting offered scope or commitments need an explicit disposition: clarify, qualify, resolve, or deliberately retain under the user's decision. Internal costs need not be disclosed.

When a requirement changes, identify actual consequences for supplier instructions, estimates and customer outputs. Update work within scope and mark uncompleted updates. Do not close an issue merely because a reply or acknowledgement arrived.

## Release packages and sensitive material

A sent package needs its exact contents, recipient, date, purpose and transmission evidence preserved. A file saved under Issued is not proof of sending; prepared-but-unsent material remains a draft. Distinguish acknowledgement from acceptance.

Separate internal pricing and discussion from recipient copies, checking hidden comments and unintended content when preparing release. Folder names do not provide access control. Existing user authorization governs sending and commitments; do not invent a new approval hierarchy.

## Filing dependencies and repeated exchanges

Use party subfolders when repeated exchanges dominate; use subject divisions only when they improve retrieval. Keep received originals and covering messages together or clearly cross-referenced. Preserve usable relative paths for native files with dependencies and associated exports.

Do not duplicate files merely to satisfy two filing schemes. An unchanged attachment can legitimately belong to several preserved historical packages; identical bytes do not make the exchanges redundant. Hashes help establish identity when needed, not correctness or approval.

## Concurrent editing and closure

When several people or sessions edit records, establish simple ownership or sequencing. Preserve disputed evidence and reconcile conflicts; do not silently choose the newest file.

At completion, record the outcome, final issued outputs and remaining obligations in the existing guide and log. Keep evidence retrievable. Do not invent retention periods or deletion policies; archiving is not a backup.
